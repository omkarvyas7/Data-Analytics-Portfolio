# Project Charter: Returns Disposition Defect Reduction (DMAIC)

Version 1.2 (re-baselined after Measure) | Data is synthetic, generated by `src/generate_data.py`. No Amazon or employer data is used.

## 1. Business case

Returned items arrive at four returns processing centers (RC-WEST, RC-CENTRAL, RC-EAST, RC-SOUTH). An associate grades each unit and assigns one of six dispositions: resell as new, open-box, refurbish, liquidate, recycle or destroy. A wrong disposition costs money in both directions. Grading too low sends resellable units to liquidation or the shredder, which loses recovery value and creates avoidable waste. Grading too high ships a used item as new, and those units come back as complaints and second returns. The project ties directly to the program's zero-defect and zero-waste goals and to cost per unit processed.

## 2. Problem statement

From July 2025 to February 2026, **6.8%** of QA-audited units (725 of 10,636) received the wrong disposition, a DPMO of **68,165** (about 2.98 sigma). Over the same eight months, misgrades left **$195,203** of recovery value unrealized across 106,136 units, sent **468** resellable units to recycle or destroy, and generated a customer complaint rate of 1.21% of units processed. RC-CENTRAL had the highest site defect rate (8.5%).

## 3. Goal statement (SMART)

Reduce the network disposition defect rate (QA-audited, weekly p-chart) from **6.8%** to **3.5% or lower** as the Control-period average by **30 June 2026**, with the process in statistical control, while (a) raising the recovery rate (achieved / potential $) from 95.7% to at least 97.0%, (b) cutting waste units per 10,000 processed by at least 50%, and (c) keeping mean dock-to-disposition time at or below 12 hours.

## 4. Scope

| In scope | Out of scope |
|---|---|
| Grading and disposition decision at 4 returns centers, all shifts | Carrier transit and customer refund timing |
| Dock-to-disposition flow (dock wait, triage, queue, grading, routing) | Pricing of resale channels and liquidation contracts |
| Grading SOP, checklist, photo step, associate onboarding | Seller-fulfilled and third-party returns |
| QA audit sampling and defect cause coding | Vendor refurbishment quality after hand-off |
| Weekly reporting and control charts | System replacement of the warehouse management system |

## 5. Critical to Quality (CTQs)

| CTQ | Customer | Measure | Spec / target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Correct disposition | Resale customer, Finance | Defect rate (audited), DPMO | <= 3.5% weekly average |
| Correct condition shown to buyer | Resale customer | Upgrade misgrades, complaint rate | Complaint rate down 40% |
| Value recovered | Finance, ReCommerce | Recovery rate = achieved / potential $ | >= 97.0% |
| Nothing resellable scrapped | Sustainability | Waste units per 10k | -50% vs baseline |
| Timely disposition | Operations | Dock-to-disposition hours | Mean <= 12 h; SLA 48 h |

## 8. Risks and assumptions

- The QA audit decision is treated as ground truth. Auditor agreement still needs checking, and an attribute agreement analysis is planned.
- The ~10% audit sample (20% at the pilot site during the pilot) is random within site and day.
- Potential recovery value comes from the ReCommerce pricing model for the correct disposition.
- Seasonal volume can confound before/after comparisons, so the pilot is read against comparison sites over the same weeks.
